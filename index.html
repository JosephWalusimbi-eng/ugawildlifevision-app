        How to use this app

        1. 1
          Choose your language

          Pick a language on the Home page or in the menu at the top. Animal names are then shown, and can be heard, in that language.

        2. 2
          Identify an animal

          Tap the orange camera button. Take a photo, or choose one from your gallery. Fill the frame with one animal and zoom in if it is far away. The app names the animal in under a second, on your phone.

        3. 3
          Check the answer

          A confidence bar shows how sure the app is. If it says "Not sure", try a closer, clearer photo. "Other possibilities" lists the next best guesses.

        4. 4
          Learn about it

          Tap "Open in guide" for photos, facts, its conservation status, a safety note and its name in other languages. Tap Listen to hear the name.

        5. 5
          Browse the guide

          The Animals tab lists every animal. Search by name in any language, or filter by group: mammals, birds, reptiles, amphibians or fish.

        6. 6
          Record your sightings

          Tap "I saw it!" on an animal's page. The Sightings tab shows what you have seen and how many animals are left to find. Sightings stay on your phone.

        7. 7
          Use it offline

          Open the app once with internet, then add it to your home screen (Android: menu ⋮ → Install app; iPhone: Share → Add to Home Screen). After that it works without a network.

        ⚠️ Stay safe

        Never approach wild animals to take a photo. Stay behind fences and follow your guide. The app is a learning aid, not a safety device.

        About this app, its limits and photo credits

        About

        • A pocket field guide to Ugandan wildlife. It works offline; the model runs on your phone and your photos are not uploaded anywhere.
        • It knows 25 kinds of animal. Anything else should come out as "Not one of the species I know", but it can still be wrong.
        • Lion, buffalo, cheetah, kudu and hyena had very few training photos. Treat those answers with caution.
        • Ugandan-language and Kiswahili names and audio come from Sunbird AI machine translation and speech; French, German and Dutch names are common names spoken by your phone's own voice. All are marked "not yet checked" until a native speaker confirms them.
